Get started

Up and running in a minute

Needs a Mac with Apple silicon on macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later.

Download

Get Inkwell.dmg, open it and drag Inkwell to Applications.

Allow access

The welcome window walks you through allowing Inkwell under Privacy & Security → Accessibility.

Pick an AI

Choose the local model Inkwell recommends, or add your own key. Then just start typing.

Inkwell isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS asks you to confirm once: open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway, or run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Inkwell.app.

Questions

Good to know

Is Inkwell really free?

Yes. It's open source under the MIT license, with no account and no subscription. Local models are free too; only cloud AI providers charge for your own API key.

Which apps does it work in?

Most Mac apps with a text field: Mail, Notes, Messages, Pages, Slack, Discord, and web pages in Safari, Chrome, Arc, Brave and Edge, including Gmail, Google Docs and Notion. A few apps don't expose their text to macOS; there Inkwell stays quiet.

Why does it need Accessibility access?

That's how macOS lets an app read the field you're typing in and put a fix back. Inkwell never reads password fields, and you can switch it off for any app or site.

Which local model should I pick?

Inkwell recommends one based on your Mac's memory. Gemma 4 E2B is a good start: fast and accurate for grammar. Very small models (1B) are fine for next-word suggestions but too small for grammar checks.

How do updates work?

Inkwell checks once a day and asks before installing. You can check now or turn automatic checks off in Settings → General.

I can't see the menu bar icon.

On MacBooks with a notch, macOS hides menu bar icons when the app in front has many menus. Open Inkwell again from Applications or Spotlight to reach its settings.
